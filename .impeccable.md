## Design Context

### Users

The site is first and foremost Xiangyu's personal space: a memorable, explorable expression of his work and interests as an AI application engineer. Its most important practical audience is recruiters and hiring managers, with technical peers and potential collaborators as a secondary audience.

Visitors should be able to understand within 30 seconds who Xiangyu is, what he builds, and where to see his strongest work. People who have more time should be rewarded with a playful, interactive exploration rather than forced through a conventional resume page.

Primary jobs to be done:

- Quickly understand Xiangyu's role, focus, and strongest projects.
- Verify that he has built real AI products, not merely collected technology keywords.
- Explore his experience, technical thinking, personality, and future direction.
- Find direct links to projects, resume, and contact information without having to complete the interactive experience.

### Brand Personality

Playful, curious, and capable.

The experience should feel inventive and personal while still giving recruiters confidence in Xiangyu's engineering judgment. It may be atmospheric and slightly mysterious, but should never become obscure, childish, or difficult to navigate.

The voice is concise, human, and confident. English is the primary language. Technical content should emphasize decisions, ownership, evidence, and outcomes rather than buzzwords.

### Aesthetic Direction

An interactive pixel-art "Cyber AI Lab" set in a future Hong Kong research space. The site uses one focused laboratory scene with a small number of meaningful interactive objects rather than a large game world.

The visual identity should avoid generic cyan-purple cyberpunk and generic AI-generated neon imagery. Prefer a more specific Hong Kong night palette: ink blue or green-black environments, warm amber and vermilion signage, and restrained cool cyan for machine or AI status. Rain, dense city windows, harbor light, bilingual environmental details, and lived-in desk objects can make the world personal.

Peter Oravec's portfolio is a conceptual reference for turning a resume into an explorable place, layering readable DOM content over an interactive scene, and providing a fast path for visitors who do not have time to explore. It is not a template to copy. Avoid reproducing its open-world movement, map structure, or game complexity.

The initial experience should be visually memorable but focused: a skippable short boot sequence, one complete laboratory room, free character movement within that room, four or five clear interactive stations, purposeful sprite animation, and an always-available quick-access route. The room should feel like a small playable place rather than a static hero image with clickable decorations.

### Design Principles

1. **Personal world, professional proof** — Atmosphere creates interest; concrete project evidence creates trust.
2. **Explore by choice** — Reward exploration, but always provide immediate access to projects, resume, and contact details.
3. **One room, free exploration** — Build one dense, intentional, genuinely walkable laboratory rather than a static stage, sprawling world map, or speculative game system.
4. **Content before spectacle** — Show the problem, Xiangyu's ownership, technical decisions, results, and evidence before adding advanced animation or live AI.
5. **Specific beats generic** — Use future Hong Kong details and personal artifacts instead of default cyberpunk and AI visual tropes.
6. **Fast and accessible underneath** — Keep content semantic, linkable, mobile-friendly, keyboard-accessible, and usable with reduced motion or without Canvas.
